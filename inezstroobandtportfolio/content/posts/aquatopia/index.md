Hack The Future is an annual hackathon organized by the **Cronos Group**, aimed at students in their final or penultimate year of an IT or creative program. The twelfth edition was centered around **Aquatopia**: a futuristic underwater world that ran as a common thread through all the challenges. I took part on day 1, the IT day, and worked with a fellow student on **Flexso's** challenge: **S.O.S. Abyss**.

The scenario was set in the year 2550: an underwater base had been attacked, and we were the only survivors. The task was to restore the damaged systems, figure out what had gone wrong, and escape in time before the danger returned. Technically, the challenge used **TypeScript**, an **MVC framework on the frontend**, and **Node.js as middleware**. On paper it was a classic full-stack setup, but in practice it was mostly a race against the clock, with a storyline that made everything feel a bit more alive.

What stood out to me immediately was how thoughtfully the challenge was put together. Flexso is a Belgian company that works on SAP implementations and digital transformation, but for this hackathon they kept the stack approachable on purpose. I liked that: the day was about solving problems and working together, not about knowing some weird tool.

I already had experience with TypeScript and Node.js from my studies, but using that combination under time pressure in an unfamiliar codebase feels very different from a class project. You quickly notice how important it is to stay calm and actually talk to your teammate, instead of both of you sprinting in different directions. That was one of the biggest learning moments of the day for me.

The presence of coaches was a real plus. When we got stuck, we could simply ask them for help without it feeling like failure. On the contrary, there was clearly an atmosphere where asking questions was normal, and the organization actively encouraged that. That is a culture I would like to see in a work environment too.

As a whole, the event made a professional and well-organized impression. The atmosphere was energetic, and the location at Antwerp Zoo made it feel unique.

I would definitely do Hack The Future again, and I would also recommend it to fellow students. Not just for the technical challenge, but also for the networking and the realistic look into how IT companies think and work. For anyone who is hesitating: the barrier is lower than you might think, and you get a lot more out of it than you expect beforehand.
