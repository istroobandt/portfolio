On November 5, 2025, I attended a Tech&Meet evening at Howest, organized by **Cyber3Lab**. The event lasted an hour and a half and focused entirely on **Flutter**, Google's **cross-platform framework**. Just like the Hugging Face session a week earlier, it was part of the series of Tech&Meet evenings that Cyber3Lab regularly organizes for students and professionals. Flutter also has an active meetup community in Belgium.

The target audience was broad: developers, designers, and tech decision-makers who wanted to understand what "build once, deploy everywhere" means in practice. You could feel that in the atmosphere too: both students and professionals were present.

The speaker was **Thijs Pirmez**, an Android and iOS developer associated with Howest Cyber3Lab. Thijs has worked at Howest since May 2023, after an earlier career as a Project Engineer at BEP Europe NV. He is a self-taught developer who mainly focuses on Flutter for app development, but he also has experience with native Android development, front-end web, and AI research, especially object detection in mobile applications. The fact that he approached the topic from real project experience, rather than purely from theory, made his presentation credible and practical.

The event was clearly technical in nature. The session covered Flutter's widget system, hot reload, integration with native code, and concrete app examples. Still, it remained accessible: even without prior knowledge of Flutter, you could follow the main ideas. I already had some basic knowledge of mobile development through my studies, but Flutter itself was mostly new to me. I found the idea of one codebase for iOS, Android, and web surprisingly powerful. Flutter makes it possible to use that single codebase directly across multiple platforms, which significantly reduces development time and maintenance.

What I learned most was how Flutter differs from native development, and why it can be a smart choice in many scenarios. The live demos helped make abstract concepts concrete. One small note: the session was compact, which meant that certain topics, such as Flutter's weaknesses or more complex integrations, were covered a bit quickly. More depth on that critical side would have made the evening even more valuable.

Would I explore Flutter further? Definitely. It fits well with my interests in app development, and the low barrier to getting started makes it appealing to build projects with it myself. Would I attend the event again or recommend it? Absolutely, especially for anyone who wants to explore the mobile or cross-platform world. It is an approachable, high-quality way to connect with real-world practice and with people in the field.
